Plant your people.
Grow a forest.

Every person you know becomes a tree. Friends cluster into groves, close friendships grow tall, and the ones drifting away turn autumn and ask for a little water. Walk around it like a farming game. Remember everyone.

Your people stay yours. Everything is parsed and stored on this device. Contacts and messages never touch a server, and you can delete everything whenever you like.

Who's tending
this forest?

Your name helps us spot you in your own data, so you don't end up as a tree in your own forest. Your city plants your home meadow.

Where should your
forest grow from?

Pick a source to open it. Start with the quick one — your forest sprouts today. The slower ones make it richer whenever they arrive.

Export your contacts

  1. Google: contacts.google.com → select all → Export → Google CSV
  2. Apple: Contacts app → select all → File → Export → Export vCard
  3. Drop the files below — .csv and .vcf both work, several at once is fine

Got both a Google export and an Apple vCard? Drop them both — people in both files are merged into one tree, not duplicated.

Request your LinkedIn export
(then forget about it)

  1. LinkedIn → Settings → Data privacy → Get a copy of your data
  2. Choose the larger archive → Request
  3. The email arrives in about a day. Come back and drop Connections.csv here

No file yet? That's normal. Your progress is saved, so come back and drop it any time.

On a Mac?
Add your real texting history.

Websites can't read your texts (good!). This helper runs on your Mac, offline — it counts who you text and when, writes one summary file, touches nothing else.

  1. Open Terminal, paste the command, press return
  2. Grant Full Disk Access if asked, run again
  3. Drop the friend-forest-messages.json it makes here
python3 helper/extract_messages.py ~/Desktop/friend-forest-messages.json

The helper lives in this repo's helper/ folder — it counts who you text and when, writes one summary file, and never uploads anything.

Plant friends
by hand

Name is all that's required — everything else just makes their tree richer. Groups (like book club or college) become groves in your forest.

Export a few
WhatsApp chats

  1. Open a chat → tap the name → Export chat → Without media
  2. Save / AirDrop the .txt to this device (unzip if needed)
  3. Repeat for your favorite people, then drop them all below

Only message counts and dates are used, never the messages themselves.

Add your social
circles

Every big platform will hand you your own data. Drop whatever arrives — follow lists become trees, message threads make them tall.

  1. Instagram: Settings → Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Download your information → JSON. Use followers/following and messages.
  2. Facebook: same Accounts Center download → JSON. Use friends.json and messages.
  3. X: Settings → Your account → Download an archive → drop following.js / follower.js.
  4. Anything else (Discord, Strava, Goodreads…): a .csv or .json with a name column works.

Unzip the export first and drop the files inside. Only names, counts and dates are read — never the messages themselves, and nothing leaves this browser.

Let Claude write
the field guide

Optional: with your own Anthropic API key, Claude writes a short living profile for your closest friends — who they are, how you probably met, what groves fit them. Skip it and you still get the full forest, just with simpler name cards.

How it works: your browser calls Claude directly with your key. No server in between, and the key is only kept for this session. The cost lands on your key — typically a few cents for about 30 profiles. You can get a key at console.anthropic.com.
Paste a key first, or choose "Skip this".
